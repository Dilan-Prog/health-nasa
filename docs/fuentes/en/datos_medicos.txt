Architecture and Clinical Specification: Health
Monitoring Software for Astronauts
Biomedical engineering guide, biomarkers, alert thresholds, and countermeasures validated by
NASA (NASA-STD-3001, OCHMO, and HRP Standards)
Official Reference Documents: NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 1 (Crew Health), OCHMO-TB-031 (Exercise Overview),
OCHMO-TB-006 (Pharmaceutical Care), Human Research Program (HRP Risks), and
ASCR Reconditioning Protocols.
1. General System Architecture
For the software to be viable in space environments (International Space Station, Artemis lunar missions,
or transit to Mars), it must operate under a hybrid Offline-First (Autonomous) architecture divided into four
functional layers:
Ingestion and Telemetry Layer: Captures continuous data from wearable devices (ECG, actigraphy), telemetry
from exercise machines (ARED, T2, CEVIS), medical diagnostic equipment (portable ultrasound, OCT), and
manual entries on a tablet.
Baseline Engine: Stores the individual pre-flight physiological profile (L-180 to L-30
days) of each crew member. All deviations are calculated as percentages relative to the crew member's own baseline.
Clinical Rules and Triage Engine (CDSS): Evaluates biomarkers in real time against NASA's mandatory
limits and classifies anomalies into severity levels (Green, Amber, Red).
Intervention and Countermeasures Module: Generates dynamic exercise prescriptions, nutritional adjustments,
pharmacological suggestions from the authorized medical kit, and step-by-step guides for medical
emergencies without communication with Earth.

A. Dynamic Exercise Prescription (OCHMO-TB-031 Standard)
Mandatory Daily Load: 2.5 hours per day (6 to 7 days per week), including warm-up,
cool-down, and equipment setup.
Resistive Training (ARED): Multi-joint exercises (squats, deadlift, bench press,
heel raises) with simulated loads of up to 600 lbs (272 kg) to stimulate osteogenesis.
Cardiovascular Training (T2 and CEVIS): 30- to 45-minute sessions alternating continuous
resistance and high-intensity interval training (HIIT) with axial-load harnesses on the treadmill.
B. Pharmacological Database (ISS Medical Kit - OCHMO-TB-006)
The support module for the Crew Medical Officer (CMO) must include real-time inventory, dosage,
contraindications, and radiation-accelerated expiration of the following groups:
Space Adaptation Sickness (SAS): Promethazine (oral/IM), meclizine, ondansetron.
Sleep Disorders and Fatigue: Melatonin, zolpidem, zaleplon (sleep inducers); modafinil,
caffeine (wakefulness promoters).
Analgesia and Inflammation: Ibuprofen, acetaminophen, aspirin, ketorolac.
Antimicrobials and Antivirals: Amoxicillin/clavulanate, azithromycin, ciprofloxacin, valacyclovir (for
reactivation of latent viruses due to immunosuppression).
Emergencies and Life Support: Epinephrine (anaphylaxis), 100 % oxygen (decompression
sickness), enoxaparin (thrombosis).
C. Post-Flight Reconditioning Protocol (45 Days)
Upon detecting the landing event (G-load > 1.0 G sustained), the software automatically switches to
ground rehabilitation mode:
Day 0 (Landing): Continuous monitoring of orthostatic intolerance, intravenous/oral rehydration, and
activation of anti-gravity compression suits.
Days 1 to 14 (Neurovestibular and Mobility Phase): 2 hours per day of physical therapy focused on balance,
basic gait, and flexibility. Strict block on impact exercises (running or jumping).
Days 15 to 45 (Strength and Power Phase): Gradual progression of functional loads, proprioception, and
aerobic reconditioning until 100 % of baseline values are restored.

5. Autonomy Levels by Mission Type
The software design must allow the mission profile to be configured according to NASA's levels of
medical care:
Care Level 3 (Low Earth Orbit / ISS): Periodic synchronization with servers on Earth (Mission Control in
Houston), synchronous telemedicine with Flight Surgeons, and medical resupply every 3-6 months.
Care Levels 4 and 5 (Artemis Missions to the Moon and Transit to Mars > 30 to 210+ days): Communication delay
of up to 20 minutes each way. Requires a local autonomous triage engine, visual/interactive guides for
emergency surgical or dental procedures, and predictive algorithms for inventory consumption.

7. Key Non-Functional Requirements for Development
Offline-First Architecture: Encrypted local database (e.g., SQLite/local PostgreSQL with differential synchronization) capable of operating indefinitely
without an Internet connection.
Low Cognitive Load Interface (Space UX): Use of high contrast (dark mode by default for cabin
lighting), legible typography at a distance, standardized color codes (Green/Amber/Red), and
quick navigation for emergency situations or use with gloves/touchscreens.
Medical Security and Privacy: End-to-end encryption (AES-256) and role-based access control
(Crew Member, Crew Medical Officer, Flight Surgeon on the ground) complying with space medical
confidentiality regulations.
